Why did I Do This

I've spent a lot of time building my own projects and troubleshooting computers. It seemed like a good way to make some extra money, while introducing them to free software.

While working on other people's computers, I noticed many people are frustrated with the software they use, but don't realize they have alternatives. They may think the browser that came with their computer is the only option, or that they have to pay for expensive software and subscriptions to get things done. I want this service to help people understand that they have more choices than they might think, including free and open-source software that they can install, control, and learn to use themselves.

I also believe we should move toward software that collects less personal information and gives users more control. The more dependent we become on a small number of massive companies for our software, files, communication, and information, the more power those companies have for surveillance, or propaganda, and the easier they can potentially be influenced by governments or other powerful interests.

Technology should work for the people using it, not the other way around. My goal is to help people understand their options, become less dependent on centralized services, and feel confident enough to take control of their own technology.

Why you should use free software, make your own gopher or IRC server, and seed torrents.

Massive data collection and the growing centralization of digital services have created privacy and security risks that some are only beginning to recognize. Today, a handful of companies control much of the software, media, news, and services people rely on. When access to information and software depends on centralized platforms, those platforms become potential bottlenecks that can be influenced by governments, corporations, or other outside interests.

Android is a good example. If most users can only get their apps through a platform controlled by one company, that company becomes a gatekeeper over what software people can access and what developers are allowed to distribute. Even if that power is never abused, concentrating it in one place creates an opportunity for censorship or authoritarian influence.

That's why free software, alternative app stores like F-Droid, and independent software distribution, like torrents, matter. People need the ability to obtain and run software directly, without depending entirely on a single corporation or government-influenced platform.

A free society needs more than freedom of speech on paper. It needs the technical freedom to access, share, and run information independently. We should preserve those alternatives now, before increasing centralization makes them much harder to maintain.

If buying isn't owning. Than sharing isn't stealing.
